Christmas do ideas: a night (or lunch) colleagues will actually remember

A Christmas do is the British work party that fills diaries from mid-November — a pub lunch in Leeds, a quiz in a Manchester function room, a jumper contest in a London office. The speeches and Secret Santa get planned. The photos vanish into fifty camera rolls. Here are Christmas do ideas that work for small teams and bigger offices, plus a simple way to keep every guest photo in one place.

Christmas do ideas that people actually join in

  • Festive quiz: short rounds (films, music, your own office lore) and a small prize on each table.
  • Christmas jumper contest: funniest, most extra, best homemade — judged by a quick show of hands, not a long awards segment.
  • Daytime lunch: easier for parents, carers and anyone who would skip a late night out. Book a private room and keep speeches short.
  • Wreath-making or a cooking class: colleagues move around instead of sitting in the same seats all evening.
  • Secret Santa with a cap: £10–£15 is the usual range; keep it optional so nobody feels boxed in.

Where the best photos happen

The doorway when people arrive in jumpers, the quiz-sheet panic, the unplanned dance after dessert. Hired coverage, if you have any, often misses the last hour — that is when colleagues take the funniest videos.

Frequently asked questions

What is a Christmas do?

In the UK, a Christmas do is the work Christmas party — a booked lunch, a quiz night or an evening out that the team plans from November onwards.

What are good Christmas do ideas for a small office?

A festive quiz, a jumper contest, a daytime lunch in a private room, wreath-making and an optional Secret Santa with a clear spending cap.

How do I collect photos from the work Christmas party?

Print a QR code on the tables and quiz sheets. Guests scan and upload photos and videos from the browser.

Do colleagues need to create an account?

No. They can upload without signing in; signed-in guests also get a private copy of their uploads.
