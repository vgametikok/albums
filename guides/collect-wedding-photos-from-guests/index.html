How to collect wedding photos from guests — without an app

Your guests will take more photos than your photographer — candid moments at the tables, the dance floor, the getting-ready chaos. Most of those photos never reach you. Here is how to actually collect them, step by step.

Why most guest photos never reach the couple

After a wedding, everyone promises to “send the photos later”. A few people do. The rest stay on phones, because sending them takes effort and nobody wants to be the one asking a week later. The usual workarounds each break somewhere:

  • Group chats compress photos and videos, and the best moments sink between voice notes and stickers. Finding a specific photo a month later is hard.
  • Hashtags only work for guests who post publicly, and you depend on a social network to keep them.
  • Apps lose people at the first step. Older relatives, guests with full phones and anyone without a password at hand simply skip it.
  • Shared cloud folders usually need an account on that service, and a folder link passed around a chat ends up in places you did not intend.

The pattern is the same every time: each extra step — install, sign up, log in — costs you a share of the guests. The method that works best is the one with the fewest steps.

The no-app method: one QR code on the tables

The simplest flow is a single QR code that opens an upload page in the phone’s browser. A guest points the camera at the sign, taps the link, picks photos and videos, and they land in your album. No app, no account, no password.

That is how an Event Album on Albums works:

  1. You create the album. A name and a few words for your guests. The printable QR sign is ready right away — put it on the tables.
  2. Guests scan it. They land on a page with your greeting. Photos and videos go into the album straight from the phone browser.
  3. You sort it out and take it home. Every photo can be shown to everyone, kept for friends, or hidden. The whole album downloads as one archive.

Taxes are included. The guest number is only a guide — guests and uploads are not counted or limited; storage is the only real limit. See all plans.

2. Write a short greeting

Guests see it when they open the link. Keep it warm and specific: “Friends, this is our wedding album. Drop in anything you took today — the candid ones especially.” A clear invitation gets more uploads than a generic “share your photos”.

3. Decide on privacy

You can turn on holding, so guest uploads wait for your approval before anyone can see them. A hidden album — visible to your guests only — works right away; an album open to everyone goes through review first.

4. Print the signs and place them well

One sign per table works better than one sign at the entrance. Add it near the guest book, the bar and the photo corner too. For ideas, read our guide to wedding QR code table signs.

5. Check the venue’s internet

Uploading needs a connection. If the signal is weak, tell guests they can scan now and upload later — the link is permanent and the upload window never closes.

On the day: small things that double the uploads

  • Ask out loud once. A ten-second mention from the host or the DJ (“scan the code on your table and add your photos”) reaches the guests who never read signs.
  • Show the photos live. A live photo wall on the venue screen shows new uploads as they arrive. When guests see their photo appear, they upload more.
  • Mention videos. Short clips of the speeches and the first dance are often the most treasured files. Video is capped at 50 MB per file — roughly a minute from a phone.
  • Let people stay anonymous. A guest’s name is never published without their say-so, which removes one more reason to hesitate.

After the wedding

Guests keep uploading for days — the upload window never closes. Then it is your turn:

  • Sort. Show to everyone, keep for friends only, or hide completely — one tap, or a whole batch at once. A guest list with counts shows who sent what.
  • Tell the story. Add a cover, chapters and your own voice notes next to the photos, so the album reads like a story rather than a dump of files.
  • Download everything. A ZIP with files by chapter and a text file of captions — readable in ten years, with or without Albums.
  • Mind the clock. Storage runs 6 months from the first upload, not from the payment, and can be extended by another 6 months.

One more thing guests appreciate: after uploading, a guest can sign in and every photo they added is saved to their own album too — in their own free account. Uploading stops being an act of giving away.

Honest limits to know

Photos are resized to 2048 px on the long side — great for screens, 10×15 prints and a photo book, not for a wall-sized poster. For large prints, ask your photographer for the originals.
