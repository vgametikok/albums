Christmas lunch ideas for a hot Australian Christmas

Start typing “christmas lunch ideas” in Australia and Google adds “australia”, “for hot weather” and “for large group”. A December 25 lunch in 30-degree heat calls for a different plan than a snowy roast dinner. Here are practical ideas for the menu, the day and the photos everyone takes on their phones.

A menu that suits the heat

  • Seafood: a platter of prawns, oysters or smoked salmon. Order early – seafood shops get very busy in the last days before Christmas.
  • Cold meats: glazed ham, sliced turkey or roast chicken served cold, so the oven is off by midday.
  • Salads: potato salad, a big green salad, mango or watermelon salad.
  • On the barbie: lamb cutlets, sausages or fish if you want something hot without heating the kitchen.
  • Dessert: pavlova with berries and passionfruit, trifle, or a Christmas pudding for the traditionalists.

Ideas for a large group

  • Make it a bring-a-plate lunch: you do the ham, everyone else brings a salad or dessert. Agree who brings what in the family group chat.
  • Keep drinks in eskies with plenty of ice and water for the kids.
  • Set up shade: a gazebo, umbrellas, a table under the trees. Put sunscreen by the door.

Little extras

  • Bonbons on every plate – the paper hats and bad jokes are half the fun.
  • Backyard cricket after lunch, a water fight or a dip in the pool for the kids.
  • A Kris Kringle so everyone gets one gift instead of buying for the whole family.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good Christmas lunch in hot weather?

Cold dishes work best: seafood, cold ham or turkey, salads and pavlova. A barbecue keeps the heat out of the kitchen.

How do you feed a large group at Christmas?

Ask everyone to bring a plate, agree on the dishes in advance and keep the host responsible for the main meat.

Do guests need to install anything to share photos?

No. They scan the QR code and upload in the browser.
