Pet Photo Album Online: A Free Album for Your Dog or Cat

Pets fill our phones. Hundreds of photos of the same dog on the same sofa, a cat in a box, a puppy's first walk. The trouble is that they drown among everything else. A pet photo album online puts them in one place, in order, with captions and short videos, and lets you share your pet's story with family and friends. This guide shows how to build one for free and what to put in it.

Why a pet photo album is worth making

  • Pets grow up fast. The puppy or kitten phase passes in a few months.
  • Your phone is a mess. Pet photos mix with receipts, documents and everything else.
  • Everyone wants to see. Family and friends love updates, but a flood of messages gets lost.
  • It becomes a keepsake. Looking back through the years is one of the nicest things an album does.

Ways to organise a pet album

By year or age

Chapters such as "Puppy: weeks 8 to 16", "First year", "Year two". The simplest, and it shows how your pet changed.

By theme

"Walks", "Sleeping", "Funny faces", "Vet visits", "Travel", "Birthday".

By season

Snow, beach, autumn leaves, summer in the garden.

One album per pet

If you have several animals, one album each keeps stories apart; you can add a shared one for family days.

Photos and videos worth keeping

  • The first day at home
  • A weight-and-size marker, such as a photo next to the same toy each month
  • Sleeping positions
  • Funny faces and bad haircuts
  • First snow, first swim, first trick
  • Meeting other pets and people
  • Vet or groomer days, for your own records
  • Short videos of barking, purring or zoomies
  • A photo of the paws, the nose or the tail: small details become favourites

Captions, names and stories

Give each photo a short caption: the date, what happened, and the personality. "Biscuit steals a sock again" tells a story that a bare photo does not. Add a chapter note for each stage of life. Over time the captions become a small biography of your pet.

Add voice and sound

A voice note can capture what photos cannot. Record in your browser the sound of your dog's snoring, your own words to the cat, or a short story about the day you brought your pet home. Play the album as a slideshow with narration for a family gathering.

How to create a pet album on Albums.ink

  1. Sign in with Google at albums.ink.
  2. Create an album named after your pet.
  3. Add chapters by year, age or theme.
  4. Upload photos and short videos, then write captions.
  5. Choose who can see it: public, friends only or private, with exceptions for specific people.
  6. Share the link with family, a vet, a pet sitter or friends. A public album opens without signing in.

Share with the people who care

  • Family: friends-only access keeps the album among the people you have added.
  • A pet sitter: a short album with feeding notes, routines and photos of your pet's habits; make it public only if the sitter has no account and the album holds nothing personal.
  • A breeder or rescue: share progress with the people who helped you find your pet.
  • Your friends: a public album can be a lighter alternative to posting every photo on social media.

A free album can have one co-author, so a partner can add photos too. Pro allows up to 10 co-authors.

A gentle note about loss

If a pet has died, an album can be a place to remember them. Gather the favourite photos, write a few lines about what they meant to you, and add a recording of your voice or a family message. Keep it private or share it with the people who loved your pet. Regular albums are stored as long as you need them.

Pro ($9.99 per month) adds 4K photos, video up to 500 MB, up to 10 co-authors and stats. Albums.ink is a website: no mobile app, no AI, no face or pet recognition, no printing and no automatic sync from your phone, and no ads.

Quick checklist

  • One album per pet, chapters by year
  • A shortlist of your best photos, not everything
  • Captions with dates and little stories
  • A few videos and a voice note
  • Privacy set the way you want

Common mistakes

  • Uploading every blurry duplicate.
  • Mixing several animals in one story without chapters.
  • Forgetting captions, so the details are lost.
  • Leaving the album public when it is meant only for family.

Frequently asked questions
