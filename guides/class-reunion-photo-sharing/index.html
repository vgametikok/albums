Class reunion photo sharing: collect then-and-now photos with one QR code

Reunions are about two sets of photos: the ones from school days that everyone half-remembers, and the ones taken tonight. A QR code album collects both, so classmates can relive the past and keep the present.

The reunion photo problem

Ten, twenty or thirty years after graduation, classmates are spread across cities and platforms. Some are on every social network, some on none. Old photos sit in albums at their parents' houses. And on reunion night, everyone takes photos that end up in private chats nobody else sees.

A shared album with one QR code works for all of them. People open the link or scan the code, and upload from the phone browser without an app or an account. Organizers own the album and keep it tidy.

Before the reunion: the throwback drive

Start collecting weeks ahead. Include the album link in the invitation email and the class group, with a clear ask: “Dig out your old photos — prom, sports, trips, the cafeteria — and add them here.” People scan old prints with their phones or take a photo of them and upload.

Create chapters by year or theme: “Freshman year”, “Teams and clubs”, “Prom”, “Graduation day”. The archive fills up before the event and becomes a reason for people to attend.

On reunion night

  • Show the live wall. Put the album on a projector or TV. Throwback photos uploaded in advance mix with photos from tonight, and the room gathers around the screen.
  • QR signs on every table and at the bar. Add a short line like “Class of 2006 — add your photos. No app.”
  • Recreate old photos. Find a group photo from school and ask the same people to recreate it. Upload both side by side.
  • Name tags with the old yearbook photo are a classic; add the QR code to the back of the name tag too.
  • Ask for videos. Short clips of speeches and the dance floor upload directly; up to 50 MB per file.

Help people find each other in photos

Encourage captions with names, especially for old photos. Twenty years on, nobody is sure who the third person on the left is. The organizers can add text and voice notes next to photos, which works well for telling the story behind a legendary class trip.

Classmates who couldn't make it

Send the link to everyone on the class list, not only those attending. People living abroad can follow the live wall and add their own old photos. Because the upload window never closes, the album keeps growing long after the night itself.

Privacy for a class album

A class album usually belongs to the class, not the internet. Keep it hidden so only people with the link can see it, and turn on approval if you want to review uploads first. Classmates can upload anonymously, and nobody's name is published without their say-so. If the link ends up somewhere it shouldn't, issue a new one; the old code stops working and existing members keep access.
