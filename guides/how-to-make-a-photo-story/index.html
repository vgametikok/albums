How to Make a Photo Story: From a Pile of Photos to a Story People Follow

Everyone has a camera roll full of photos; very few people have photo stories. A photo story is a small set of photos arranged so they tell something: a trip, a day, a person, a change. Instagram and Facebook stories vanish after a day; a photo story you build as an album can last for years. This guide explains how to make a photo story step by step, how to structure it, how to add voice narration, and how to share it with friends for free on Albums.ink.

What makes a photo story different from an album

An album collects photos. A photo story chooses and orders them so that each one adds something. It has a beginning that sets the scene, a middle with the main moments, and an ending that closes the story. Captions and voice connect the dots.

Step 1: Decide what the story is about

Write it in one sentence before you choose any photos:

  • "Our first trip with the baby, and how nothing went to plan."
  • "How grandpa's garden changes over one year."
  • "The day we moved into our first home."

If you cannot say it in one sentence, the story is probably two stories.

Step 2: Choose the photos

  1. Collect all candidates in one place.
  2. Remove duplicates and blurry photos.
  3. Keep photos that move the story forward.
  4. Aim for 15 to 40 photos for a short story; more if you use chapters.
  5. Include a few short videos for motion and sound.

Step 3: Build the structure

Opening

One or two photos that set the scene: a wide view, a sign, a departure.

Middle

The main moments in order. Mix wide photos, close-ups and people. Each photo should show something new.

Turning point

Most good stories have one: rain on the wedding day, a missed train, a first step.

Ending

A calm closing photo: a sunset, a sleeping child, a closed door, a group hug.

Step 4: Use chapters for longer stories

Chapters make a long story easy to follow. On Albums.ink you can split a story album into chapters, for example by day, place or stage. Each chapter can start with a short narration that sets the scene.

Step 5: Add words and voice

  • Captions: one line, telling what the photo cannot show: a name, a feeling, a fact.
  • Voice notes: place a short clip next to a photo to tell the moment in your own voice.
  • Voice narration: narrate the story album so viewers hear you as they go.

Viewers can play the story album as a slideshow under your narration or the voice notes on each photo. Voice notes can be recorded right in the browser or uploaded. Voice is what makes a photo story personal. Tone, laughter and pauses say more than text.

Step 6: Pick a cover and a title

The cover is the first thing people see when you share the link; on Albums.ink it appears in the link preview. Choose a photo that hints at the story, and a title that sparks curiosity: "Six days, four flat tyres" works better than "Iceland 2026".

Step 7: Share it

Set privacy: public, friends only or private, with exceptions for specific people and visibility per photo. Share by link, or let friends and followers find it in their feed. They can like, comment and bookmark it.

Photo story ideas

  • A day in the life: morning to night in 20 photos.
  • Before and after: a renovation, a garden, a haircut, a weight-loss journey.
  • A person: a portrait story of a grandparent with their narration.
  • A trip: chapters by day with evening voice notes.
  • A project: building, cooking, crafting from idea to result.
  • A year: twelve chapters, one per month.
  • An event: wedding, birthday or graduation from preparations to the last dance.

Common mistakes

  • Too many similar photos.
  • No clear ending.
  • Long captions that repeat what the photo shows.
  • Voice notes that run for minutes. Keep them short.
  • Forgetting people. Faces make stories memorable.

Photo stories vs. social media stories

Social media stories are quick and vanish after 24 hours. A photo story album on Albums.ink stays, can be updated, has chapters and narration, and can be kept private. Many people post a quick story on social media and link to the full photo story album.

A quick example

Say the story is "our first camping trip with two kids". The opening is the overloaded car and the first view of the lake. The middle covers setting up the tent, the first swim, cooking on the fire and a muddy football game. The turning point is the storm on the second night, with a voice note from your partner laughing in the dark. The ending is a calm morning photo of the kids asleep in the tent. Twenty-five photos, three short videos, two chapters and four voice notes: a story friends will watch to the end.

Frequently asked questions

How do I make a photo story?

Decide the story in one sentence, choose 15 to 40 photos, order them with a beginning, middle and end, add captions and voice, then share.

How many photos should a photo story have?

Usually 15 to 40 for a short story. Use chapters for longer ones.

Can I add narration to a photo story?

Yes. On Albums.ink you can add voice notes next to photos and voice narration to story albums.

Do photo stories on Albums.ink disappear?

No. Story albums stay until you delete them.

Can I make a photo story on my phone?

Yes, in the phone browser. There is no app to install.
