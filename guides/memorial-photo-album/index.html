Memorial photo album: gathering photos of a loved one from family and friends

When someone dies, the photos of their life are spread across many people's phones and shoeboxes. A shared album lets family and friends contribute memories easily, without anyone having to chase them.

Why a shared album helps

Families planning a memorial often need photos quickly — for a slideshow, a memory board or the order of service — at a time when nobody has the energy to collect them one by one. Friends from different parts of the person's life each hold a few photos the family has never seen: a colleague's retirement party, a fishing trip, a university reunion.

A QR code album makes contributing simple. People scan the code or open the link and upload photos from their phone browser. There is no app to install and no account to create, which matters when many of those contributing are older friends and relatives.

Before the service

Create the album and share the link

Give the album the person's name and a short message, for example: “We are gathering photos of Margaret to celebrate her life. Please add any you have — old or recent.” Include the link in the funeral notice, the family email or the message sharing service details.

Ask for context

Encourage people to add a caption: when the photo was taken and who is in it. These details are easy to lose and precious later.

Review before sharing

Turn on approval so every photo waits for the family before anyone else sees it. Some photos may be painful, unflattering or simply not right for the occasion, and approval lets you decide quietly.

Funeral and memorial photo display ideas

  • Place a small QR card near the guest book and on the memory table, with a line like “Share a photo or memory of Margaret.”
  • Mention it once, briefly, in the closing words: many guests have photos on their phones and will add them that day if invited.
  • Consider a slideshow. The live photo wall can run on a screen during the reception, showing photos as they are approved. Families often find it comforting to see new memories arrive.
  • Help older guests. Ask a grandchild or friend to help anyone who would like to scan old prints with a phone and upload them.

Voice and stories, not only photos

The album owner can add voice notes and text next to photos. A daughter explaining the story behind a photo, or a recording of a eulogy, gives the album depth that pictures alone cannot. Chapters such as “Early years”, “Family”, “Work and friends” and “The celebration” help the album read like a life story.

Privacy and dignity

  • Keep the album hidden, so only people with the link can see it.
  • Use approval to review each upload.
  • Guests can upload anonymously; no name is shown without their consent.
  • If the link spreads further than you want, issue a new link. The old one stops working; people already in the album keep access.

Keeping the memorial photo album

Memories keep arriving for weeks or months — someone finds an envelope of prints, a friend abroad hears the news late. The upload window never closes, so they can still contribute.
