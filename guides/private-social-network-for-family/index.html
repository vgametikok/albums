Private Social Network for Family: A Free Space Just for Your People

Families used to share news on big social networks, then drifted into a dozen group chats. Neither feels right: public networks show your kids to strangers and advertisers, and chats lose everything after a week. A private social network for family sits in between: a feed, comments and likes like the networks you know, but only for the people you invite, with photos kept in albums instead of disappearing. This guide explains what a family network needs, the options, and how to set one up for free on Albums.ink.

What a family social network should do

  • Invite-only audience. Relatives and close friends, nobody else.
  • A feed. New photos and albums appear without anyone needing to ask.
  • Reactions. Likes and comments so grandparents can say "beautiful!".
  • Albums, not just posts. Holidays, birthdays and years kept together.
  • Fine privacy. Some albums for everyone, some only for parents.
  • Simple sign-in. No complicated accounts for older relatives.
  • Lasting storage. Photos should still be there in 20 years.

The options families use

Big social networks with private settings

Facebook groups and private Instagram accounts are familiar, but the platforms are built for public reach and advertising, and privacy settings change over time.

Group chats

Private and instant, but photos are compressed, buried and hard to find later.

Dedicated family apps

Some apps focus on babies and close family. They can be lovely but often require everyone to install them, and some features are paid.

Album-based social networks

Albums.ink is a free website with friends, follows, a feed, likes, comments with likes and bookmarks, built around albums with privacy per album, per person and per photo.

How Albums.ink works as a family network

Setting it up in one evening

  1. Create your account with Google on Albums.ink.
  2. Make a first album to set the tone: last holiday, a birthday or old family photos.
  3. Send the link to your family chat. It shows a cover preview.
  4. Ask relatives to sign in with Google and add you as a friend.
  5. Agree on privacy: kids' albums friends only or private, public only for things you would put on a fridge door.
  6. Invite a co-author for shared albums. Free albums allow one co-author from your friends.
  7. Encourage comments. The first few comments make others join in.

Keeping it private

Friends-only albums are visible to your friends on Albums.ink, minus anyone you exclude. Private albums are visible only to you and the people you add as exceptions. Inside any album, individual photos and videos can be hidden. This lets you share a birthday album with the whole family while keeping a few photos just for parents.

Getting grandparents on board

  • Sit with them once and sign in together on their phone browser.
  • Save the site to their home screen like an app icon.
  • Post albums with voice notes; hearing grandchildren is the best motivation.
  • Ask them to narrate old photos: they become storytellers, not just viewers.

Family traditions that work well

  • Sunday album: one person posts the week's best photos.
  • Birthday album: everyone adds a photo and a comment for the birthday person.
  • Year in review: a story album with twelve chapters every December.
  • Family history: old photos with grandparents' voice narration.

Is it really a social network?

Yes, in the way that matters: profiles, friends, follows, a feed, likes and comments. The difference is the focus on albums and stories and the control over who sees what. You can also make albums public and follow people outside the family if you like.

What it costs

Albums.ink is free with unlimited albums and posts. Free photos are stored at up to 2048 px. Pro ($9.99/month) keeps photos up to 4K, allows videos up to 500 MB, up to 10 co-authors per album and stats. Most families never need Pro.

Honest limits

There is no mobile app, only the website. There is no automatic phone sync, no AI, no face recognition and no printing. The interface is available in 9 languages, which helps families spread across countries.

Rules that keep a family network friendly

  • Agree that kids' photos stay friends only or private.
  • Ask before posting photos of other people's children.
  • Keep comments kind; it is a family space, not a debate club.
  • Let each household have its own albums, and use co-authors for shared events.
  • Download important albums once a year as a family backup.

How it differs from a group chat

A group chat is a stream; a family network is a library. In a chat, last month's photos are buried under hundreds of messages. On Albums.ink, every album stays in its place with comments, voice notes and chapters, and you can bookmark favorites to find them again. Use the chat to talk and the network to keep.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best private social network for family?

Look for invite-only sharing, a feed, comments, albums and fine privacy. Albums.ink offers all of these for free on the web.

Do relatives need to install an app?

No. Albums.ink is a website; relatives sign in with Google in their browser.

Can I keep kids' photos away from strangers?

Yes. Use friends-only or private albums, add exceptions only for trusted relatives and hide individual photos if needed.

Is there a cost?

Unlimited albums and posts are free, and your albums stay as long as you want them. Pro is optional.

Can relatives in other countries use it?

Yes. It works in any browser and the interface is available in 9 languages.
