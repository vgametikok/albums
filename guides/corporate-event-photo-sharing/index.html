Corporate event photo sharing: how to collect attendee photos with a QR code

Your attendees take hundreds of photos at every conference, offsite and holiday party. With one QR code you can collect them in a single moderated album and actually use them for recaps, internal channels and next year's promotion.

The problem with attendee photos at work events

A hired photographer covers the stage and the official moments. The rest — teams laughing at dinner, the workshop whiteboards, the people who flew in from three offices — lives on attendees' phones. Asking for those photos in Slack or by email gets a handful of replies, usually after the recap post has already gone out.

Corporate settings add some extra constraints:

  • No new accounts. Many companies restrict installing apps on work phones, and nobody wants another login.
  • Moderation matters. Not every photo from the after-party belongs in a public recap.
  • Mixed audiences. Clients, partners and employees are in the same room, so access has to be simple and controlled.

The QR approach

A QR code on badges, table tents, the stage screen and the registration desk opens an upload page in the browser. Attendees pick photos and videos from their gallery and they land in one album owned by the organizer. No app, no account, no IT ticket.

With an Event Album on Albums you can turn on approval, so every upload waits for your review before anyone sees it. That keeps the album clean without asking attendees to think about what is appropriate.

Setting it up for different corporate events

Conferences and summits

Put the QR code on the holding slide between sessions and on the back of the badge. Ask the MC to mention it before the keynote and after the last session. Create chapters for each day or track so the album stays organized as hundreds of photos arrive.

Team offsites and retreats

Smaller groups, more candid moments. Share the link in the welcome message and put a sign at breakfast. Offsite photos make the best material for internal newsletters and onboarding decks, so collect them while people are still excited.

Holiday parties and company celebrations

Use approval mode, and consider keeping the album hidden so only attendees with the link can see it. A live photo wall on a screen near the bar is one of the easiest ways to make a company party feel lively.

Product launches and client events

Here the photos also serve marketing. Collect everything, approve the best, then download them for social posts and the event recap. Always check permissions with people who appear prominently before publishing outside the company.

Getting attendees to actually upload

  • Make the ask concrete. “Share your three favourite photos from today” works better than “share your photos”.
  • Show results live. A live wall at a coffee break proves the album is real and fills up quickly.
  • Keep names optional. Attendees can upload anonymously, which lowers hesitation for people who don't want to be identified.
  • Send one follow-up. The day after, a short email with the same link catches people who were too busy during the event.

Privacy and brand safety

Corporate event albums deserve a few guardrails:

  • Use approval so nothing appears until you have reviewed it.
  • Choose a hidden album, visible only to people with the link, for internal events.
  • If the QR code leaks beyond the audience, issue a new link: the old code stops working and people who already joined keep access.
  • Mention in the event information that attendee photos may be used in internal recaps, and honour opt-outs.
