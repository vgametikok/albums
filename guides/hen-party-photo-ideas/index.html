Hen party photo ideas: props, challenges and one album for every hen's photos

A hen do produces hundreds of photos — on eight different phones, in three group chats, half of them never seen by the bride. These hen party photo ideas cover props, a photo challenge, group poses and the part most hens forget: getting every picture into one private album the bride can keep.

Hen party photo props that actually get used

  • Bride-to-be sash and veil — the classic, and it makes the bride easy to spot in every group photo.
  • Matching items for the hens — sunglasses, T-shirts or a colour code, so the photos look like one weekend.
  • Speech-bubble boards with in-jokes: “Last fling before the ring”, “She said yes, we said Prosecco”.
  • A frame prop with the bride's name and the date — great for the first group photo of the weekend.
  • A Polaroid-style frame on a stick for cheeky close-ups.

Keep props in one bag that travels with the organiser; props left at the Airbnb are props nobody uses.

A hen party photo challenge list

A photo challenge keeps the group moving between venues and gives quieter hens a reason to join in. Split into teams and give points for each photo uploaded to the album before midnight:

  1. The bride with a stranger wearing a hat
  2. Every hen in one photo — taken by a passer-by
  3. Recreate a photo from the bride's childhood
  4. The bride ordering a drink in a funny accent (video counts double)
  5. A selfie with a bartender, a bouncer and a taxi driver
  6. The whole group mid-jump
  7. Something borrowed, something blue — found on the night
  8. A toast to the groom, filmed for him to see later

Scoring is easy when every team uploads to the same album: the organiser just scrolls through and counts.

Group poses for a hen do

  • The “bride in the middle” lineup on a staircase — everyone visible, nobody hidden at the back.
  • Walking towards the camera in a row, arms linked.
  • Everyone looking at the bride while she looks at the lens.
  • A sitting circle on a picnic blanket, phone held above the group.

How to collect every hen's photos in one place

Group chats compress photos, and someone always says “I'll send them later” and never does. An Event Album on Albums solves it with one QR code:

  1. The organiser creates the album before the weekend and names it — “Sophie's Hen Do, Brighton”.
  2. Share the QR code in the group chat and put it on a card at the table or the cottage door.
  3. Hens scan with the phone camera and upload photos and videos in the browser. Nothing to install, no account needed.
  4. Turn on Hold guest photos if you want to approve what appears in the album — useful for the photos the bride's mum shouldn't see.
  5. After the weekend, download the whole album as one archive or share a private link with the bride.

Frequently asked questions

What props do you need for a hen party photo?

A bride-to-be sash and veil, matching sunglasses or T-shirts for the hens, a named frame prop and a few speech-bubble boards are enough for a group of 8–15.

What is a hen party photo challenge?

A list of photos for teams to capture during the night — the bride with a stranger, a recreated childhood photo, a toast to the groom. Points go to the team that uploads the most to the shared album.

How do hens share photos without a group chat?

Use a QR code album: hens scan it and upload from the phone browser without an account, so every photo and video lands in one place in full size.

Can I stop some hen party photos from being seen?

Yes. With Hold guest photos switched on, uploads wait for your approval, and you choose who sees each photo.
