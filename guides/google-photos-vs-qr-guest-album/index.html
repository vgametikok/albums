Google Photos shared album vs a QR guest album for your wedding

A shared album in Google Photos is the default many couples reach for. It is free and familiar — but a wedding is a special case: hundreds of guests, one evening, and photos you want to keep for decades. Here is how the two approaches compare.

The short version

A shared cloud album works well for a small group of friends who already use the same service. A QR guest album is built for the opposite situation: many guests, mixed devices and ages, and no time to explain anything. The difference comes down to how many steps stand between a guest and the upload button.

Guest experience

With a shared cloud album, guests generally need an account on that service to add photos, and often the app. At a wedding that is where you lose people: grandparents, guests from abroad, anyone with a full phone or a forgotten password.

With a QR guest album like an Event Album on Albums, guests need nothing but a camera. They point the phone at the sign on the table, the upload page opens in the browser, and photos and videos go straight into the album. No Google account, no app, no sign-up. Fewer steps means more guests actually upload.

Privacy and control

A shared link in a group chat travels further than you planned, and in a shared folder everyone with access sees everything as it arrives. For a wedding you usually want more control. An Event Album gives you:

  • Three states for every photo — show to everyone, keep for friends only, or hide completely.
  • Approval first, if you like — with holding on, guest photos wait for your approval and nobody sees them until then.
  • A guest list with counts — how many each guest sent, how many are shown and how many are waiting.
  • A replaceable code — if the link reaches the wrong people, issue a new one; the old QR stops working and guests who joined stay.
  • Limited guest rights — a guest deletes only their own uploads and cannot edit the album.
  • Anonymity by default — a guest’s name is never published without their say-so.

Organization: a feed vs a story

A cloud album is a long scroll of files in upload order. That is fine for backup, less so for remembering the day. An Albums album can have a cover, chapters and your own voice notes next to the photos — a story you can read again. On the day itself, a live photo wall can show new uploads on the venue screen.

Storage and cost

A general cloud account comes with a storage quota tied to the owner’s plan; a few hundred guests uploading photos and videos can fill it quickly, and keeping everything usually means paying for storage on an ongoing basis.

An Event Album is a one-time purchase per event instead of a storage subscription: Small $39.99 (100 GB, recommended for up to 100 guests), Medium $69.99 (200 GB, up to 250 guests) or Large $129.99 (400 GB, up to 500 guests), taxes included. Guests never pay. Storage runs 6 months from the first upload — not from the payment — and can be extended by another 6 months. Ran out of space? You can buy more.

Keeping the photos for good

Whatever you use, make sure you can get everything out. With Albums the whole album downloads as one archive: a ZIP with files by chapter and a text file of captions, readable in ten years with or without Albums. Guests benefit too: after uploading, a guest can sign in and every photo they added is saved to their own album as well, in their own free account.

Quality notes

Be aware of the trade-offs on each side. On Albums, photos are resized to 2048 px on the long side — great for screens, 10×15 prints and a photo book, not for a wall-sized poster — and video is capped at 50 MB per file, roughly a minute from a phone. For poster-size prints, ask your photographer for the originals.

Which should you choose?

If your guest list is a dozen close friends who all use the same cloud service, a shared album may be all you need. If you expect dozens or hundreds of guests of every age, want control over what is visible, and want an album that reads like a story, a QR guest album is the better fit. You can also try Albums for free first: story albums are free, and you can create one to see how it feels before your event.
