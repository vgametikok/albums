How to Add Voice to Photos Online: Narrated Albums for Free

A photo shows what happened. A voice tells you how it felt. Adding voice to photos turns a silent gallery into a story: a grandmother explaining who is in a 1970s picture, a child describing their favorite day of the holiday, a friend laughing about the trip that went wrong. This guide explains the ways to add audio to photos, from video editors to narrated albums, and shows how to do it online for free with voice notes and voice narration on Albums.ink.

Ways to add voice to photos

Turn photos into a video

Video editors and slideshow apps let you place photos on a timeline and record a voiceover. The result is a video file. It is great for a finished film, but editing takes time, and changing one photo later means exporting again.

Record a voice memo and send it with the photo

The quick way in a chat. It works, but voice memos and photos get separated and lost in the conversation.

Narrated albums

An album where voice notes sit next to the photos they describe, and narration guides viewers through chapters. Nothing to render or export: you add and change things any time. This is how Albums.ink works.

How voice works on Albums.ink

  • Voice notes: record one right in the browser or upload an audio file, and attach it to a photo.
  • Voice narration: record album narration in the browser or upload it, so viewers hear you as they move through the story.
  • Slideshow playback: the album plays as a slideshow under your narration or the voice notes of each photo.
  • Chapters: split long stories into parts, each with its own narration.
  • Mixed media: photos, videos and voice notes together in one album.

All of this is free, with unlimited albums kept as long as you want.

How to add voice to photos, step by step

  1. Sign in to Albums.ink with Google in any browser.
  2. Create an album and upload the photos and videos.
  3. Arrange them in the order of the story and split them into chapters.
  4. Record a voice note for the photos that need explaining.
  5. Add narration at the start of each chapter: where, when, who and why.
  6. Listen back once from start to finish.
  7. Choose privacy and share the album by link or with friends.

What to say in a voice note

Silence in front of a microphone is common. These prompts help:

  • "This was taken in..., we were there because..."
  • "The person on the left is..., and what you can't see is..."
  • "What I remember most about this day is..."
  • "The funny thing that happened right after this photo was..."
  • "If I could go back to this moment, I would tell myself..."

Keep each note under a minute. Short notes get listened to; long ones get skipped.

Ideas for narrated photo albums

Family history

Ask grandparents to narrate old photos. Their voices, names and stories become a family treasure that a caption could never replace.

Travel

Record a short note each evening of a trip. Months later, your own voice brings the place back.

Children growing up

A yearly album where the child describes their year in their own words. Listening to a five-year-old and then a ten-year-old is priceless.

Gifts

A narrated album for a birthday, anniversary or farewell: friends add photos, and you record the story of your friendship.

Memorials

A gentle way to keep a loved one's memory, with photos and the voices of the people who loved them.

Recording tips for better audio

  • Record in a quiet room; soft furnishings reduce echo.
  • Hold the phone 15 to 20 cm from your mouth.
  • Speak a little slower than normal.
  • It is fine to laugh, pause or restart. Natural beats perfect.
  • For older relatives, ask questions and record their answers.

Privacy for personal recordings

Voices are personal. On Albums.ink each album can be public, friends only or private, with exceptions for specific people, and individual photos and videos can have their own visibility. Keep family stories private or share them with relatives only.

Why voice beats a caption

A caption explains. A voice carries tone, accent, humor and emotion. Years later, families often say the recordings matter more than the photos, because they are the only place a loved one's voice survives.

What to know before you start

Albums.ink is a website; there is no mobile app, but you can record voice notes right in a modern phone or computer browser, or upload audio files. There is no AI voice, no automatic transcription and no printing. Free photos are stored at up to 2048 px on the long side, which looks sharp on screens.

Keeping a copy

You can download a whole album at any time, so you always have your own copy of the photos alongside the online album.

A 20-minute project to try this weekend

Pick one old photo that matters to your family and one person who remembers it. Sit together, open the photo on a screen and ask three questions: who is in it, what happened that day, and what happened next. Record the answers as three short voice notes and put them next to the photo in a new album. Add two or three related photos, a chapter title and a cover. Share the album privately with relatives. This small test usually starts a chain reaction: someone remembers another photo, someone else adds a comment with a forgotten name, and a single photo becomes the first chapter of a family story.

Frequently asked questions
