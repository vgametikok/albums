Shared photo album for iPhone and Android: what actually works

Half your family has an iPhone, the other half Android. Here is how to make one shared photo album that everyone can actually add to.

Can iPhone and Android share a photo album?

Yes, but not every option works equally well. iCloud Shared Albums are built for Apple devices; Android users can only view a public web link, not upload. Google Photos works on both, but everyone needs a Google account and the app. A browser-based QR album works on any phone with no account.

iCloud Shared Albums with Android users

Turn on “Public Website” in the album to give Android users a viewing link. They still can't add their own photos, which defeats the point for a party or family album.

Google Photos shared album on iPhone and Android

Works on both platforms, but every contributor needs a Google account and usually the app. Older relatives and guests at events often get stuck at sign-in.

One album for every phone

Guests scan a QR code and upload from the browser — iPhone or Android, no app, no account.

Frequently asked questions

Can Android users add photos to an iCloud shared album?

No. They can view a public website link but cannot upload.

What is the best shared photo album for iPhone and Android?

For events and mixed groups, a browser-based QR album works on any phone without an app or account; Google Photos works if everyone has a Google account.

Do guests need an app?

Not with a QR event album: they upload from the phone browser.
