Shared photo album for iPhone and Android: what actually works

Half your family has an iPhone, the other half Android — and every time you try to share photos, someone can't open the album, can't upload, or ends up with blurry compressed images from a chat. This guide explains which shared photo album options actually work across iPhone and Android, step by step, and which one to pick for a family, a party or an event.

Can iPhone and Android share a photo album?

Yes, but not every option works equally well. Here is the short version:

  • iCloud Shared Albums are built for Apple devices. Android users can only view a public website link; they cannot add photos.
  • Google Photos works on both iPhone and Android, but every contributor needs a Google account, and usually the app.
  • Cloud folders (Google Drive, Dropbox, OneDrive) work everywhere but are file managers, not albums, and often need sign-in to upload.
  • Browser-based albums with a link or QR code work on any phone with no app and no account.

The right choice depends on whether people only need to view photos or also add their own.

How to share an iCloud album with Android users

  1. On iPhone, open Photos → Albums → your Shared Album → People.
  2. Turn on Public Website and copy the link.
  3. Send the link to Android users; they can view and download photos in a browser.

The limitation: Android users can't add their own photos. For a family where everyone contributes, that defeats the point.

How to create a Google Photos shared album on iPhone and Android

  1. Install Google Photos on iPhone (it's built in on most Android phones) and sign in.
  2. Create an album and tap Share.
  3. Invite people or create a link; turn on collaboration so others can add photos.

This works well if everyone already has a Google account. Problems appear with older relatives, guests at events and anyone who doesn't want another app. For a detailed comparison with a QR album, read Google Photos vs a QR guest album.

Why group chats are not a photo album

WhatsApp, Messenger and similar apps are how most families share photos today, but they compress images, bury them between messages, and leave out anyone not in the chat. They are fine for quick sharing, not for keeping memories.

Common problems and fixes

  • “I can't open the iCloud link on Android.” Make sure Public Website is turned on; the link opens in Chrome but upload is not possible.
  • “Grandma can't sign in to Google.” Use an album that doesn't require an account.
  • “Videos won't play.” Large videos may still be processing; wait a few minutes after upload.
  • “Photos look blurry.” Avoid forwarding through chats; upload the originals from the camera roll.
  • “Too many albums.” Agree on one link for each event and pin it in the family chat.

Photo quality and storage

  • iCloud Shared Albums store reduced-resolution copies.
  • Google Photos depends on your storage settings and plan.
  • Chats compress heavily.
  • Albums stores photos in up to 4K (3840 px on the long side) — great for screens, standard prints and a photo book — and you can download everything as one ZIP with captions.

Privacy

Whatever you choose, check who can see and who can add. With an Event Album, keep it hidden so only people with the link can open it, use approval for large groups, and change the visibility of any photo at any time.

Which shared album should you choose?

  • Everyone has an iPhone → iCloud Shared Album is fine.
  • Everyone has a Google account and is comfortable with apps → Google Photos.
  • Mixed phones, older relatives, or many guests → a browser QR album.
  • You want a story with voice, not just files → Albums.

No. Android users can view a public website link of an iCloud Shared Album but cannot upload.
