Baby shower photo album: collect every guest photo with one QR code

A baby shower photo album is one of the first keepsakes a child will ever have: the gifts, the games, the grandparents-to-be, the friends who came to celebrate. The hard part is gathering the photos, because the parents-to-be are busy opening presents while everyone else takes the pictures. This guide shows how to collect every guest photo into one album with a QR code, plus baby shower photo ideas, games that get people uploading, and how to turn it all into a lasting keepsake.

What goes into a baby shower photo album?

A good album tells the story of the day rather than just collecting files. Aim for:

  • Arrivals and decorations — the backdrop, balloon arch, diaper cake and table settings before guests mess them up.
  • The parents-to-be — candid photos laughing with friends, not only posed ones.
  • Gift opening — reactions matter more than the gifts themselves.
  • Games — the funniest moments, the winners, the guessing results.
  • Generations — grandparents-to-be, great-grandparents, aunts and cousins together.
  • Guest messages — advice cards, wishes for the baby, recorded advice.
  • The cake and food — before anyone cuts it.
  • Group photo — one photo with everyone who came.

How to set up a baby shower photo album

  1. Create an event album with a warm name: “Welcome, Baby Rivera” or “Sophie's baby shower”.
  2. Write a greeting guests see when they open the upload page: “Thank you for celebrating with us! Please add any photos you took today.”
  3. Choose privacy. Keep the album hidden so only people with the link can open it, and turn on approval if you'd like to check photos before others see them.
  4. Print the QR sign. It is ready as soon as the album exists. Test it with an iPhone and an Android phone.
  5. Share the link early. Add it to the invitation so guests can upload their own baby photos for a guessing game.

Guests upload from the phone browser with no app and no account, which matters at a shower where the guest list often spans three generations.

Where to put the QR code

  • The gift table — gift opening is when phones come out.
  • The dessert table or diaper cake — the most photographed spot in the room.
  • Next to the guest book or advice cards — guests are already leaving something behind.
  • Each seating table — small signs on tables beat one big sign at the door.
  • The photo backdrop — guests have just taken a photo and are ready to share it.

Baby shower photo ideas and games

Prompts give guests a reason to take — and share — photos. Mix and match:

Photo ideas

  • Mom-to-be (or both parents) under the balloon arch
  • The bump with each grandparent-to-be
  • A photo of every guest holding a tiny outfit or shoe
  • Close-ups of hands writing advice cards
  • The parents reading their favourite card
  • Siblings-to-be with the cake
  • A “before and after” of the dessert table

Games that double as photo prompts

  • Photo scavenger hunt. Give guests a list: the funniest gift, someone crying happy tears, the parents laughing, the cake before it's cut. The first to upload them all wins.
  • Baby photo match. Ask guests to upload their own baby photos before the shower, open the album on a TV and guess who is who.
  • Guess the bump. Photograph each guest's yarn guess against the bump and reveal the closest.
  • Advice in a voice note. As the album owner you can add voice notes next to photos — record a grandparent's best parenting tip and place it beside their photo.

Virtual and long-distance guests

Send the album link to relatives who can't attend. They can browse the album from home and add their own photos and greetings. Since uploads stay open after the event, a grandmother overseas can add a photo of the blanket she knitted weeks later. Turning the photo album into a keepsake

After the shower:

  1. Sort photos. Show the best to everyone, keep some for family only, hide blurry ones.
  2. Add chapters: “Decorations”, “Gifts”, “Games”, “Family”.
  3. Add a cover — the group photo or the parents with the cake.
  4. Add voice notes to tell the story behind key moments.
  5. Download the ZIP with captions to keep your own copy and print a photo book.

Photos are stored in up to 4K (3840 px on the long side), which is great for screens, standard prints and a photo book. Later you can continue the story — the hospital, the homecoming, the first birthday — in a free story album with photos and recorded voice.

Baby shower photo album vs a printed photo book

They work best together. The online album collects everything from every guest; the printed book is a curated selection for the shelf. Collect first, pick the best 40–60 photos, and print them. If you want a physical guest book too, leave space for the QR code on its first page so visitors can add photos later.

Frequently asked questions

How do I collect photos from baby shower guests?

Place a QR code at the gift table and on each table. Guests scan it and upload photos from the phone browser into one album, with no app or sign-up.

What should be in a baby shower photo album?

Decorations, candid photos of the parents-to-be, gift opening, games, family generations, guest messages, the cake and a group photo.

What are good baby shower photo ideas?

The bump with each grandparent-to-be, guests holding tiny outfits, the parents reading cards, a photo scavenger hunt and a baby photo guessing game.

Can relatives who can't attend see the photos?

Yes. Send them the album link and they can browse the album and add their own photos and greetings.

Can I keep the baby shower album private?

Yes. Keep the album hidden so only people with the link can open it, and turn on approval to check uploads first.
