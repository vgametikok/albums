How to collect photos from event guests with one QR code

Every event ends the same way: hundreds of great photos are taken, and the organiser gets a handful. This guide explains how to collect photos from event guests — at a wedding, party, conference, team-building day or company celebration — with the easiest method we know: one QR code that leads to one shared album.

Why collecting photos from event guests is hard

The problem is not that guests don't take photos. It is that every photo ends up in a different place: personal camera rolls, private chats, Instagram stories that disappear after a day. Collecting them afterwards means chasing people one by one.

Common approaches and why they fail:

  • “Send me your photos” emails — a few people reply, most forget.
  • Group chats compress images and don't include everyone.
  • Hashtags only work for public posts on one social network, and many guests don't post at all.
  • Shared cloud folders often require accounts; guests give up at the login screen.
  • Event apps need a download that most guests won't bother with for one evening.

The easiest way to collect photos from a group: one QR code

The method that works best is to remove every step you can:

  1. Create one event album with a guest upload page.
  2. Print its QR code on signs at the event and share the link in invitations.
  3. Guests scan with their phone camera, the upload page opens in the browser, they pick photos and upload — no app, no account.
  4. Photos land in one place, where you can approve, sort and download them.

Because uploading takes seconds and happens during the event, you collect photos while guests are still enthusiastic, not a week later. If you need help with the code itself, see how to make a QR code for photo sharing.

Step by step: how to collect photos from event guests

Before the event

  • Create the album and give it a clear name: “Acme Summer Offsite 2026” or “Anna & Tom's Wedding”.
  • Write a greeting that tells guests what to upload: “Add any photos and short videos from today.”
  • Choose moderation. For public or corporate events, turn on approval so nothing appears until you check it.
  • Print QR signs and test them on iPhone and Android.
  • Add the link to invitations, the agenda, the event website or a pre-event email.

During the event

  • Put signs on every table, at registration, near the stage and by the bar or coffee point.
  • Show the QR code on screen between talks or during dinner.
  • Show the QR code on a TV or projector between talks; a visible reminder is the strongest prompt.
  • Ask the host or MC to mention it once or twice.

After the event

  • Send one follow-up message with the link: “Last chance to add your photos.”
  • Review uploads, hide anything off-brand or unflattering, and split the album into chapters.
  • Download everything as a ZIP with captions.

Corporate event photo sharing: conferences, offsites, parties

Corporate events need the same simple flow plus a little more control.

Conferences and meetups

Print the code on badges and the welcome slide. Attendees capture slides, speakers and networking moments that the official photographer misses. Use approval so only suitable photos become visible.

Team-building days and offsites

Multi-day offsites benefit from chapters — one per day or activity. Share the link in the team chat before departure so photos from the trip there are included too.

Holiday parties and company celebrations

Show the QR code on the screen during the party. Afterwards, keep some photos visible to everyone and hide the rest; staff remain comfortable and the album stays brand-safe.

Product launches and client events

Approval mode lets the marketing team pick photos before anything is visible, then download the best for social media and the website.

How to get guests to upload their photos

Even the easiest system needs a nudge. These tactics consistently raise uploads:

  • Place codes where phones are already out: tables, photo booths, the stage, the bar.
  • Say “no app needed” on every sign. This one line removes the main objection.
  • Upload the first photos yourself so the album isn't empty.
  • Make it visible. A QR code on the big screen turns uploading into part of the entertainment.
  • Give a reason. “Help us make the event recap” or “We'll pick the best photo for next year's invitation.”
  • Run a small contest — best team photo, funniest moment.
  • Follow up once the next morning while memories are fresh.

Privacy and moderation

  • Approval mode: guest photos wait until you approve them.
  • Visibility per photo: show to everyone, keep for friends, or hide.
  • Hidden album: not listed anywhere; only people with the link can open it.
  • Consent: for work events, tell attendees that photos may be shared internally and let people ask for removal.
