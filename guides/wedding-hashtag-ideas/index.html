Wedding Hashtag Ideas: Examples, Formulas and How to Make Your Own

A wedding hashtag gives guests one tag to use when they post, so you can find their photos later and the day has its own little corner of social media. A good one is short, easy to spell and clearly yours. This guide gives you simple formulas, dozens of examples you can adapt, a way to check that a hashtag is unique, and an honest look at what a hashtag can and cannot do for collecting photos.

What makes a good wedding hashtag

  • Short: ideally under 20 characters, so it fits on signs and is easy to type.
  • Easy to spell: avoid unusual spellings and double letters that cause typos.
  • Unique: not already used by hundreds of other couples.
  • Readable: capitalise each word, like #AnnaAndTomSayIDo, so it reads clearly.
  • Personal: your names, surname, a shared joke or the place you met.

Simple formulas that work

Names and surnames

  • #[Name]And[Name]: the simplest option, for example #AnnaAndTom. Works best when the combination is rare.
  • #[Name]Weds[Name]: classic and clear, for example #EmmaWedsLiam.
  • #The[Surname]Wedding: uses the shared surname, for example #TheMillerWedding.
  • #Team[Surname]: short and friendly, good for guests who know you by your surname.

Wordplay on the surname

  • #HappilyEver[Surname]: a fairy-tale twist, for example #HappilyEverMiller.
  • #[Surname]EverAfter: works well with short surnames, for example #ParkerEverAfter.
  • #Finally[Surname]: for couples who have been together for years, for example #FinallyAParker.
  • #[Surname]PartyOfTwo: playful and easy to remember.

Date and place

  • #[Names][Year]: the year makes almost any hashtag unique, for example #AnnaTom2027.
  • #[Names]In[City]: great for city weddings, for example #AnnaAndTomInVienna.
  • #[Surname]At[Venue]: names the venue, for example #MillersAtTheVineyard.
  • #IDoIn[Place]: perfect for destination weddings, for example #IDoInTuscany.

Examples you can adapt

Replace the names with your own:

  • Classic: #AnnaAndTom2027, #TheMillerWedding, #EmmaWedsLiam
  • Playful: #HappilyEverMiller, #FinallyAParker, #MillerTimeForever
  • Romantic: #ForeverAndAlwaysAnnaTom, #OurLoveStoryInRome
  • Destination: #MillersInMallorca, #TomAndAnnaDoBali
  • Funny: #TomPutARingOnIt, #AnnaGotTomed, #NoMoreSingleTom
  • Short: #AnnaTom27, #TheMillers27

When a surname rhymes or sounds like a common word, use it: those hashtags are the easiest to remember.

How to check that your hashtag is unique

  1. Search it on Instagram, TikTok and Facebook before printing anything.
  2. If it already has many posts, add the year or your initials.
  3. Read it in lowercase too: some word combinations form unwanted words.
  4. Ask two friends to type it from memory; if they misspell it, simplify.
  5. Use one main hashtag; a second one only causes confusion.

One hashtag for the whole wedding weekend

Many weddings now last longer than one day: a welcome dinner, the ceremony, the party and a brunch the next morning. Use the same main hashtag for all of it, so every photo ends up in one place. If you want to separate the events, sort photos into chapters in your album instead of inventing more hashtags. Guests remember one tag; three tags mean nobody uses any of them correctly.

How to get guests to use your hashtag

  • Print it in large letters; small text on a menu is easy to miss.
  • Explain what it is for: "Share your photos with #AnnaAndTom2027".
  • Put it on the screen during the reception, next to the live photo wall.
  • Ask the bridesmaids, groomsmen or close friends to use it first.
  • Remind guests the day after in a short thank-you message, with the album link for photos they did not post.

Where to display your wedding hashtag

  • On the wedding website and the invitations.
  • A sign at the entrance and near the bar.
  • Table cards or menus.
  • The photo booth or the reception screen.
  • Mentioned once by the host during the evening.

Combine the hashtag and a QR code on the same sign, so guests who do not post publicly still have a way to share.

What a hashtag can't do

A hashtag is fun, but it is not a photo collection system:

  • Private accounts are invisible. Many guests post from private profiles, so their photos never appear under the tag.
  • Many guests never post. Older relatives and people who avoid social media keep their photos on their phones.
  • Quality is reduced. Social networks compress images and crop them to their formats.
  • Videos and stories disappear. Stories vanish after 24 hours.
  • Downloading is painful. There is no simple way to save every tagged photo at full quality.

Frequently asked questions

How do I create a wedding hashtag?

Combine your names or surname with a word like "weds", "ever after" or the year. Keep it short, capitalise each word and check that it is not already used.

Should a wedding hashtag include the year?

Adding the year is the easiest way to make a common hashtag unique, for example #AnnaAndTom2027.

Where should I put the wedding hashtag?

On the invitations, the wedding website, a sign at the entrance, table cards and the reception screen.

Will a hashtag collect all our wedding photos?

No. Photos from private accounts and guests who do not post will be missing. A QR album collects photos from everyone.
