Free Photo Blog Sites: How to Start a Photo Blog People Follow

A photo blog is a simple idea: you share photos regularly, with a few words, and people follow along. In practice, many photo blogging platforms are built for writers or for selling portfolios, and many free plans come with limits. This guide compares the main kinds of free photo blog sites, explains what makes a photo blog worth following, offers post ideas, and shows how Albums.ink works as a free photo blog where every album is a post with followers, likes and comments.

What a photo blog needs

  • Photos first: large, clean display without clutter.
  • Short text: a title and a few lines, not an essay.
  • Regular posts: an easy way to publish often.
  • Followers: people who see new posts without checking.
  • Interaction: likes and comments.
  • A simple address or link to share.

Kinds of photo blog sites

Blogging platforms

WordPress, Blogger and similar platforms can host photo blogs with themes. They are flexible, but text-first, need setup, and the best photo themes or custom domains are often paid.

Portfolio builders

Portfolio sites make work look professional for clients. They usually cost money and focus on showcasing rather than regular posting.

Social photo apps

Instagram and similar apps have huge audiences, but posts are single images or carousels in an algorithmic feed.

Photography communities

Flickr and others are great for photographers; free plans have upload limits.

Album-based social sites

How to start a photo blog on Albums.ink

  1. Sign in with Google and set up your profile.
  2. Decide your theme: a city, street photography, food, nature, family life, a hobby.
  3. Publish your first album with 10 to 20 of your best photos and a clear title.
  4. Make it public so anyone with the link can see it, no sign-in needed; visitors sign in with Google to like, comment or follow.
  5. Share the link on your other social profiles and with friends.
  6. Post regularly: weekly is enough.
  7. Reply to every comment; that is how followers stay.

Photo blog post ideas

  • Photo walks: one neighborhood per post.
  • Seasons: the same place in spring, summer, autumn and winter.
  • Series: doors, windows, coffee cups, street signs.
  • Behind the photo: a voice note on how you found the scene.
  • Projects: 30 days, 52 weeks, one photo a day.
  • Travel: chapters by day with short notes.
  • Food: recipes and dishes with step-by-step photos.

Writing for a photo blog

Keep titles specific and curious. One to three lines per album are enough: where, what caught your eye, and why it matters. Use voice notes for longer stories. Let the photos carry the post.

Growing an audience

  • Post consistently on the same day.
  • Follow people with similar interests and comment thoughtfully.
  • Share album links with cover previews where your audience already is.
  • Use chapters for long series, so people can dive in.
  • Check stats with Pro to see which albums are viewed most.

Owning your work

You can download a whole album at any time. Keep your originals in your own archive too.

A sample publishing plan for the first month

  • Week 1: an introduction album with your 15 best photos and a short voice note about what you love to photograph.
  • Week 2: a photo walk in your own neighborhood, one chapter per street.
  • Week 3: a series album built around one subject, such as doors, reflections or morning light.
  • Week 4: a behind-the-photo album: five images, each with a voice note on how it came together.

After a month you have a small but coherent photo blog, a few followers and a sense of which albums people like and comment on most.

Public, friends only or both

A photo blog is usually public, but nothing stops you from keeping some albums friends only, for example family photos, while your street photography stays public. Each album has its own privacy setting, with exceptions per person and visibility per file.

Photo blog or portfolio?

A portfolio shows your best finished work, mostly to clients. A photo blog shows a journey: experiments, walks, travels and everyday observations, mostly to people who enjoy following along. You can use Albums.ink for both by keeping one "best work" album pinned in your mind as the portfolio and publishing regular albums as the blog.
