Class reunion ideas: activities, displays and one album for every classmate's photos

Planning a high school reunion means finding people, picking a venue and giving everyone something to talk about besides “you haven't changed a bit.” Here are class reunion ideas that work for a 10-year as well as a 50-year reunion, plus a simple way to collect every photo your classmates take that night — and the old ones they dig out of the attic.

Start with the basics

  • Find classmates early: a class Facebook group, the alumni office and word of mouth. Six to nine months ahead is realistic.
  • Pick a date that works around homecoming weekend or a holiday, when many people are back in town.
  • Set a ticket price that covers food and the venue, and say clearly what it includes.

Venue ideas

  • The school gym or cafeteria — nostalgia does half the work.
  • A local brewery, winery or restaurant back room for a relaxed evening.
  • A park pavilion or backyard picnic for a family-friendly reunion with kids.
  • A homecoming game plus an after-party, so people can drop in.

Activities and icebreakers

  • Name tags with senior photos — the single most useful idea at any reunion.
  • “Who's who” quiz: match baby or yearbook photos to classmates.
  • Superlatives, updated: “Most likely to have moved the farthest”, “Most grandkids”.
  • Memory wall: cards where people write a favorite teacher, a prank or a song from senior year.
  • In memoriam table with photos of classmates who have passed away.

Ideas by reunion year

10 and 20 year reunions

Keep it casual: a bar or brewery, a playlist from your senior year and a photo challenge (the old lunch table, the class clown with the principal). Many people bring partners, so name tags with maiden names help.

30 and 40 year reunions

Add a then-and-now display, a short slideshow of yearbook photos and time for a group photo per sports team or club.

50 year reunions

Choose an accessible venue, earlier hours and plenty of seating. A printed list of attendees, an in memoriam table and a chance for classmates to share a short story are often the highlights.

Frequently asked questions

What do you do at a class reunion?

Catch up, look at old photos, play a yearbook quiz or superlatives game, remember classmates who have passed and take a group photo.

How far in advance should you plan a class reunion?

Six to nine months is realistic, so you have time to find classmates and book a venue.

How do you collect photos from a class reunion?

Use a QR code album: classmates scan the code and upload photos and videos in the browser without an account, and everything lands in one album.

Can classmates add old photos too?

Yes. Anyone with the QR code or link can upload scanned prom or yearbook photos alongside the photos from the night.
