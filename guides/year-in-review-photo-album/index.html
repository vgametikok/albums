Year in Review Photos: How to Make a Photo Album of Your Year

Every December people look back at their year in photos. Phones and apps offer automatic recaps, but a year in review you make yourself is more personal: you choose the moments, write what they meant and add your own voice. This guide shows how to build a year in review photo album in an evening, with a simple structure, caption ideas and a free way to share it with family and friends.

Why make a year in review yourself

  • You decide what mattered. An automatic recap picks faces and places; you pick the quiet day that changed things.
  • It gives the year a shape. Twelve months, a few chapters and a closing note turn a pile of photos into a story.
  • It is easy to share. One link goes to parents, friends or the group chat.
  • It becomes a tradition. One album per year gives you a row of years to scroll through later.

Start early, not on December 31

Set aside an hour in early December. If you wait until the last day, you will rush. A good order of work is:

  1. Gather photos from your phone, your camera and the group chats.
  2. Delete obvious duplicates and failed attempts.
  3. Pick a small shortlist per month, around five to ten photos.
  4. Choose one favourite from each month for a "best of" chapter.
  5. Write captions and a closing note.

Choose a structure

By month

Twelve chapters, January to December. It is the simplest structure and the easiest to read.

By theme

Chapters such as "Trips", "Family", "Friends", "Work", "Home" and "Small wins". Best if your year had a few clear storylines.

By season

Four chapters with a handful of photos each, good for a short, light recap.

Top 12

One photo for each month, with a longer caption. This works well when you want to share in a message or post.

What to include

  • A big event: a move, a wedding, a new job or a new baby.
  • Trips, even short ones.
  • Everyday life: your desk, your street, your meals.
  • People you spent time with, especially those you saw rarely.
  • A funny failure or an unexpected moment.
  • Something you were proud of, such as a message, a result or a certificate.

Captions and prompts

Short captions are enough. Try one of these for each chapter:

  • The best thing that happened in this month.
  • Something I learned.
  • Who I spent time with.
  • A song or a meal that I will remember from this month.
  • One thing I would do again, and one I would not.

At the end, write a closing note: three things you are grateful for and one goal for next year.

Add your voice

A voice note makes a recap feel like a conversation. Record a short note in your browser for each chapter or for a few key photos, then play the album as a slideshow with narration. Grandparents and friends far away often enjoy hearing your voice more than reading long captions.

A simple schedule

  • Early December: collect and shortlist.
  • Mid December: write captions and record voice notes.
  • Last week of December: share the link with family and friends.
  • January: add a "what happened next" chapter if something big comes up.

Common mistakes

  • Waiting until the last day.
  • Including every photo instead of the best ones.
  • Writing no captions, so nobody remembers the context later.
  • Forgetting the ordinary moments that made the year feel like yours.

Frequently asked questions

How do I make a year in review photo album?

Gather your photos, shortlist five to ten per month, group them into chapters, add captions and share the album by link.

Is there a free way to make a year in review?

Yes. A free Albums.ink story album supports chapters, captions, voice notes and sharing by link.

How many photos should a year in review have?

Around 60 to 120 photos works well, or just 12 if you make a top-12 version with one photo per month.

Can my family add their photos?

Yes. Free albums allow one co-author, and Pro allows up to 10.

Can I keep my year in review private?

Yes. Set the album to private or friends only, or share a public link with whoever you choose.
