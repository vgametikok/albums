Photo Slideshow for a Wedding Reception: Ideas, Length and Music

A wedding slideshow is one of the few moments when the whole room looks at the same screen and laughs, sighs and cheers together. Done well, it tells your story in a few minutes. Done badly, it drags on while guests check their phones. This guide covers how many photos to use, how long it should run, how to pick music, which free tools work, and how to collect photos from family and guests for a slideshow you make yourself.

Two kinds of wedding slideshow

Most receptions use one or both of these:

  • The story slideshow. A prepared video of your photos, from childhood to engagement, played once at a set moment, often after dinner or before the first dance.
  • The thank-you slideshow. Made after the wedding from photos guests uploaded, then sent to everyone or shown at a day-after brunch or first anniversary.

The first is about your past; the second is about the day itself. Both need the same thing: all the photos in one place.

How many photos and how long?

The most common mistake is too many photos. Plan on roughly 5–7 seconds per photo, so:

  • 3 minutes (one song): about 30–35 photos
  • 6–8 minutes (two songs): about 60–80 photos
  • 10 minutes: about 100 photos, the upper limit for most rooms

For a story slideshow, 6–8 minutes is the sweet spot. Beyond ten minutes attention drops, especially once guests have had a drink. If you have more photos you love, put them in the thank-you slideshow or the online album instead.

A structure that works

Think of the slideshow as a short film with chapters:

  1. Childhood: 8–12 photos each, alternating between the two of you.
  2. Teenage years and friends: the funny haircuts, school trips and the friends who are in the room.
  3. How you met: the first photo together, early dates, the places that mattered.
  4. Life together: trips, pets, the first home, family moments.
  5. The proposal and engagement: end on a strong, happy image.
  6. A closing card: "Thank you for being here", your names and the date.

Order photos by time within each chapter. Mixing in family members and guests keeps everyone watching, because people love seeing themselves on the big screen.

Choosing music

  • Use one or two songs for the whole slideshow; changing music every minute feels choppy.
  • Start slower for childhood and build energy towards the proposal.
  • Pick songs that mean something to you, but check the lyrics fit a family audience.
  • Fade the music out at the end instead of cutting it.
  • If you post the video online later, music licensing rules may apply; at a private reception this is rarely a concern.

Free tools to make the slideshow

You do not need special software. Popular options:

  • Google Photos: select photos, create a movie, add music. Quick and simple.
  • iMovie (iPhone, iPad, Mac): free, with transitions, titles and precise timing.
  • Canva: browser-based templates with text cards and music.
  • PowerPoint or Keynote: set each slide to advance automatically and export as a video.
  • CapCut and similar mobile editors: good for fast edits on a phone.

Whatever you use, export a video file (MP4) and test it on the actual screen at the venue.

Collecting old photos from family

The hardest part is often finding the photos. Parents have prints in boxes, siblings have files on old phones, friends have the embarrassing ones. Instead of chasing everyone by message:

  1. Create a shared online album and send the link or QR code to family and close friends.
  2. Ask for specific chapters: "baby photos", "school years", "photos of us together".
  3. Ask parents to photograph old prints with their phone in good daylight, flat and without glare.
  4. Set a deadline about three weeks before the wedding so you have time to edit.

Guest photos for a thank-you slideshow

Guests take photos you will never see unless you collect them. Here is how it works with an event album:

  1. Create an album and print its QR code on table cards and a sign at the entrance.
  2. Mention it in a short announcement or on the invitations so guests know where to upload.
  3. Guests scan, choose photos and videos, and upload from their phone browser.
  4. Sort photos into chapters and hide any you do not want others to see.
  5. After the wedding, download everything as one ZIP and import the best photos into your slideshow tool.

A thank-you slideshow made from guests' photos shows the day from angles your photographer never saw, and it makes a lovely gift to send everyone. For more on choosing a tool, read our guide to the wedding photo sharing app.

When to show each slideshow

  • Before the wedding: a short teaser from the story slideshow on the wedding website or with the invitations.
  • After dinner or before the first dance: the story slideshow, played once with the lights dimmed and the room quiet.
  • After the wedding: the thank-you slideshow of guest photos, sent with your thank-you message or played at a brunch.

Ask the DJ or host to announce the story slideshow, and do a full test run with the venue's sound and screen the day before.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Too many photos and a slideshow over ten minutes.
  • Low-resolution images that look blurry on a big screen.
  • Never testing the file, sound or screen at the venue.
  • Only photos of the couple and none of the guests.
  • Music too loud for speeches or too quiet to hear.

Quick checklist

  • Decide on a story slideshow, a thank-you slideshow, or both
  • Collect photos from family at least three weeks ahead
  • Choose 60–80 photos and one or two songs
  • Export as MP4 and test on the venue screen
  • Print QR codes for the album and download everything afterwards

Frequently asked questions

How long should a wedding slideshow be?

About 6–8 minutes, or one to two songs. More than ten minutes usually loses the room.

How many photos should a wedding slideshow have?

Allow 5–7 seconds per photo: around 30–35 photos for 3 minutes and 60–80 for 6–8 minutes.

When should the slideshow be played at the reception?

After dinner or just before the first dance works best for the story slideshow. A thank-you slideshow of guest photos fits a day-after brunch or a message to guests.

How do I get guests' photos for a slideshow?

Use an event album. Guests scan a QR code and upload from the browser; you download everything as a ZIP and add the photos to your slideshow.

What is the best free way to make a wedding slideshow?

Google Photos, iMovie, Canva or PowerPoint all work. Export as MP4 and test it at the venue.
